# Recognition

Canonical memory for target-conditioned pronunciation assessment, scoring
signals, model candidates, and evaluation requirements.

## Problem framing

The expected Japanese sentence and kana reading are known. The system does not
need unrestricted speech transcription. It needs evidence for:

1. whether the expected sentence was spoken;
2. whether it was complete and ordered correctly;
3. which regions probably differ from the target;
4. whether product policy should count the attempt.

A decoded ASR transcript is useful but insufficient. A recognizer's language
model can infer the expected sentence even when pronunciation is weak.

## Candidate local-first pipeline

```text
microphone
→ voice activity and recording-quality checks
→ lightweight Japanese recognition
→ constrained comparison with the expected sentence
→ acoustic alignment
→ localized feedback
```

Candidate free browser baseline:

```text
small local Japanese recognizer
+ expected-sentence matching
+ MFCC or log-mel features
+ dynamic time warping
+ conservative pass thresholds
```

Neither recognition nor acoustic distance should independently claim exact
phonetic diagnosis.

## Candidate approaches

### Small local ASR

- Vosk's small Japanese model is the first proposed low-cost baseline.
- Potential signals: text, confidence, timestamps, missing/substituted regions.
- Benefits: offline operation, no per-attempt fee, relatively small model.
- Risks: lower accuracy, confidence not calibrated to pronunciation, weak
  phonetic localization, and unknown Android memory use.
- Constrain recognition by the known sentence when the runtime supports it.

### Local ONNX model

- A Japanese CTC or transducer model could expose token probabilities and
  alignment through a shared browser/native model format.
- WebGPU may accelerate it.
- Risks include download size, startup time, memory, conversion/custom
  operators, and incomplete WebGPU support.
- A heavier ONNX model may fit an optional enhanced package rather than the
  default runtime.

### Direct acoustic alignment

- DTW over MFCC, log-mel, pitch, energy, or compact speech embeddings may
  localize missing regions, pauses, timing, long vowels, small `っ`, and rhythm.
- It is sensitive to reference/learner voice differences and TTS artifacts.
- Plain acoustic comparison cannot reliably identify every phonetic error.
- Pause and duration evidence need explicit modeling because DTW can stretch
  around inserted time.

### Later target-conditioned model

```text
16 kHz audio
→ compact Japanese speech encoder
→ kana or phoneme probabilities
→ constrained alignment to expected reading
→ per-unit pronunciation and timing scores
```

Its central research risk is calibration on non-native speech. Native Japanese
ASR data alone does not define which learner errors humans should accept,
reject, or explain.

## Candidate scoring signals

- content completeness;
- sentence match and recognition margin;
- duration and timing differences;
- pause placement;
- long-vowel timing;
- normalized aligned acoustic distance;
- recording quality;
- rhythm and pitch as coaching evidence.

Feedback units may be words, phrases, kana, morae, or phonemes. Granularity must
not exceed alignment reliability.

## Evaluation requirements

Evaluate with native speakers, beginner and intermediate learners, varied voice
ranges, laptop and Android microphones, quiet and moderately noisy rooms, and
slow and natural speaking rates.

Measure:

- model download size;
- peak memory;
- initialization time;
- evaluation latency;
- battery and thermal impact;
- false acceptance of weak/incorrect attempts;
- false rejection of understandable attempts;
- stability and usefulness of localized feedback.

General ASR character error rate is not the primary product metric. The
important outcome is consistent, useful decisions and feedback across repeated
learner attempts. Human pronunciation judgments are required for calibration.

A private GPT Sites session contains real user WebM recordings that can supply
an initial plumbing and transcription test after authorized recovery and
conversion. They are not yet inventoried or human-labeled and therefore cannot
calibrate acceptance thresholds or feedback quality. See
[Recordings](recordings.md).

## Open questions

- What model download and Android memory limits are acceptable?
- Is the roughly 48 MB Vosk model useful enough for the core loop?
- Is browser-TTS reference comparison stable enough across voices and devices?
- Is WebGPU merely an optimization or a distinct enhanced recognition mode?
- What can the limited tier honestly provide without a recognizer?
- Which learner population and human reference process define quality?
- How many speakers and devices are required before thresholds are trusted?
- Is false acceptance or false rejection more harmful for the learning loop?
