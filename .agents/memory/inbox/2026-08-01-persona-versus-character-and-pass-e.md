# Persona casts; Character writes. Gate gains Pass E

2026-08-01, from the user during the office-gig authoring run. The user asked
whether we design a character's gender, then made the sharper point: **the
persona affects the dialogue, so it has to be defined and its alignment
enforced** — and separately, that it must drive the visual appearance too.

The flow had the split backwards. `Persona` (apparent gender, age band, timbre)
was introduced after the taproom's male-drawing/female-voice mismatch and was
explicitly scoped to casting only: the doc said "Persona is *who is heard and
seen saying it*." Nothing connected who a character *is* to what they *say*,
and no check existed. `Voice fingerprint` covered grammar and register but not
motive.

Now, in `.agents/documents/stage-design-flow.md` and
`.agents/documents/character-separation-gate.md`:

- **Persona** = who is seen and heard. Casting instruction for art and voice.
- **Character** (new required card field) = who they are in two or three words,
  plus what those words do to their lines. At least one consequence must be
  stated as a **prohibition**, because that is the only form a gate can test:
  "a line of hers that hedges, apologises, or angles is wrong" is checkable;
  "friendly" is not.
- **Pass E — persona alignment** is the gate's fifth pass, run in both
  directions: every line must trace to a declared trait, and every declared
  trait must reach at least one line. A trait with no line is decoration, and
  worse than useless — the art work reads the same card and will draw a trait
  the dialogue never earned.
- Step 7's brief template now requires a **trait → what it looks like** table
  instead of a clothing list, plus the traits' *negative space*: what must not
  be drawn because a line already says it. A character who says she plays
  guitar must not be holding one; the drawing would make her line redundant.
- Step 9 gains a **character agreement** check — read the lines while looking
  at the drawing. The existing persona-agreement check only compares gender and
  age, so a drawing can agree on both and still be a different person.

Pass E earned itself on its first run. 「じゃあ、一緒に行きましょう。」 versus
「ぜひ、一緒に行きましょう。」: じゃあ is procedural, ぜひ is warm and slightly
insistent, and only ぜひ belongs to a character declared as practical but never
cold. Passes A–D were indifferent between them.

Two declared traits were cut for reaching no line: the coworker's "a little
self-deprecating about her own playing", and the learner's "notices details
others miss" (which duplicated an existing trait).

**Not limited to office-gig.** The café and taproom cards predate the Character
field and carry no traits, so neither has been through Pass E — see
[[2026-08-01-cafe-and-taproom-cards-predate-character-field]].
