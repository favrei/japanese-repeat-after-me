# Conversation Rehearsal UX

## Product intent

- The primary experience is repeated rehearsal of immediately useful Japanese
  phrases.
- The goal is practical spoken recall through repetition, not a grammar course
  or a conventional learning sequence.
- A learner may acquire useful phrases, such as ordering a beer or asking for
  a refill, well before studying the underlying Japanese.

## Scene model

- Present the material as a two-party conversation.
- The other party's bubbles appear on the left.
- The learner's party bubbles appear on the right.
- The scene background supplies situational context, such as a group talking
  or counter staff.
- Material submitters may provide the background. If they do not, they choose
  one of the application's defaults during submission.

## Bubble motion and pacing

- Conversation bubbles enter from the bottom and leave through the top.
- The visual feeling should resemble a music rhythm game, but the pace is much
  slower and supports deliberate speaking.
- The flow stops on the active learner bubble when the learner makes an error;
  it is not a continuously timed conveyor.
- A learner turn resolves and the conversation advances after either:
  - one correct attempt; or
  - three errors.

## Conversation rounds

- Recommend ten complete conversation rounds for a practice session.
- Ten rounds is guidance, not a hard mastery rule.
- The user may choose a different amount.

## Intro

- A scene may have an intro mode.
- The intro autoplays the conversation before asking the user to join.
- Practice begins when the user joins after the demonstration.

## Still open

- Whether the intro counts as a round.
- What the interface does immediately after the third error.
- Whether difficult turns are automatically scheduled earlier in later rounds.
- When Japanese text, reading, translation, or situational cues are visible.
- Whether rounds restart immediately or pause briefly.
- The exact motion, reduced-motion behavior, reference-audio requirements, and
  background media format.
